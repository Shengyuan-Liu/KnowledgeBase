# Paired chunk-size experiment: 256 versus 512 characters

Follow-up: [evidence-validated regrading](2026-09-21-judge-calibration.md) retains
these original scores but finds the new reviews incomplete and still semantically
unreliable. The tables below describe the original grading round only.

## Decision

Keep the application's 256-character default. In this one paired development-set
experiment, 512-character chunks increased annotated character recall from
**53.35% to 62.22%**, but lost relevant evidence on three questions and produced
material answer regressions. The recorded LLM strict pass rate rose from
**14/25 to 16/25**, but inspection found false passes and inconsistent scoring.
That score difference is not sufficient evidence for promoting the candidate.

This follows the [baseline failure audit](2026-09-20-baseline-failures.md).
All statements concern the frozen local corpus, not current NYU guidance.
Qualitative observations below are Codex inspections, not human ratings or
replacement scores. Original answers and judge responses remain unchanged.

## Results

| Metric | 256 control | 512 candidate | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Successful generation | 25/25 | 25/25 | 0 |
| Annotated character recall, 20 answerable cases | 53.35% | 62.22% | +8.87 percentage points |
| Full evidence recall, same 20 cases | 44.17% | 47.50% | +3.33 percentage points |
| Strict passes, LLM judge | 14/25 (56%) | 16/25 (64%) | +2 cases / +8 percentage points |
| Answer-point score, LLM judge | 74.33/100 | 75.33/100 | +1.00 point |
| Answerable strict passes, LLM judge | 10/20 | 13/20 | +3 cases |
| Unanswerable strict passes, LLM judge | 4/5 | 3/5 | −1 case |
| Mean retrieved excerpt characters per question | 1,280 | 2,537 | +1,257 |
| Question latency P50 | 1.203 s | 1.266 s | +0.063 s |
| Question latency P95 | 1.724 s | 1.740 s | +0.016 s |

Character recall improved on 9 answerable cases, declined on 3, and tied on 8.
Full evidence recall measures complete annotated spans, not the fraction of
fully answerable questions. Both retrieval metrics average within each question
before averaging across the 20 successful answerable cases. Unannotated alternative
passages can be valid; zero annotated coverage does not necessarily mean no useful
context, as the industry case below illustrates.

The chunk-size change also changed stride from 192 to 448 characters and nearly
doubled retrieved text. Character totals sum the five excerpts, including any
overlap, and exclude prompt wrappers, filenames, and questions. They are not token
counts or cost measurements. This is an experiment on the whole chunk-size setting,
not an equal-context-budget test of boundary quality. Timings exclude ingestion and
judging and are not a controlled performance benchmark.

## Evidence-backed changes

Source numbers are local to each question and run. Offsets are half-open character

### Improvements and remaining gaps

- **Policy scope:** candidate source [5], conduct policy `1344:1856`, contains the
  complete scope statement. The answer now includes undergraduate/graduate students,
  all schools and locations, Study Away Sites, and Portal Campuses. Annotated
  coverage rises from 0% to 100%.
- **Track names:** candidate source [1], tracks `448:960`, includes both track names
  and the change deadline. The answer replaces the incorrect Industry Concentration
  track with Biomedical Informatics. Coverage rises from 50% to 90%; the remaining
  missing annotation prefix does not prevent these facts from being present.
- **GRE submission:** candidate source [5], admissions requirements `3584:4096`,
  includes the upload destination and official-score instruction, ending with the
  truncated school name “Arts and Sci”. The answer now includes both instructions;
  the control only says to upload an unofficial-score PDF. Coverage rises from
  69.79% to 99.23%. Both receive a judge pass, obscuring the control's omissions.
- **Working while enrolled:** candidate admissions FAQ [2], `1792:2304`, retrieves
  the recommendation of six or fewer credits for full-time workers. General FAQ [3],
  `1792:2304`, starts with the truncated summer exception but omits its preceding
  continuous-enrollment rule. The candidate answers the workload and evening-class
  points and refers to the summer exception; the antecedent remains incomplete.
  Its added 10–15 hours claim comes from [3], although the paragraph cites [2].
  Treat the judge's blanket full citation/grounding score cautiously.
- **Degree credits:** candidate curriculum [2], `448:960`, supplies 36 credits,
  correcting the control's unsupported 30. But bulletin [5], `6272:6784`, cuts the
  sample plan during semester three. The answer invents a three-semester structure
  and omits the course-availability caveat; this question remains failed.

### Regressions and unresolved misses

- **GPA:** control general FAQ [3], `2304:2560`, supports both 3.0 GPA and 66%
  completion, and the answer states them with a single sentence-end citation.
  Candidate retrieval loses this passage entirely and returns admissions/link
  excerpts; the answer substitutes application requirements. Coverage falls from
  100% to 0%. Both are marked failed only because the judge rejects the control's
  valid citation placement, hiding a real regression in the pass/fail delta.
- **Career advising:** the control correctly identifies Wasserman. Candidate
  career-development [5], `896:1408`, supplies advising topics but not the center's
  name. Candidate [1] is a general resources introduction. The answer incorrectly
  identifies CDS as its own collaborator. Annotated coverage increases from 50%
  to 71.33% while this required fact is lost: mean span coverage is not completeness.
- **Industry requirements:** candidate bulletin [1], `4032:4544`, supplies DS-GA
  1009, first-year timing, three credits, and the start of the two-elective rule.
  These are useful alternative evidence despite 0% annotated coverage. It cuts
  before the six-credit total, and the candidate no longer retrieves the mandatory
  internship statement. The answer omits the two-elective count, six credits, and
  mandatory first-year internship. The judge nevertheless gives the internship
  point full credit and attributes it to [5], which is only the page introduction.
- **CPT source comparison:** control retrieval includes the admissions FAQ's
  paid-internship-for-credit context. Candidate [1], industry `448:960`, retains the
  first-year internship and OGS instructions, but no source contains the admissions
  FAQ's after-first-year rule. The answer echoes that part of the question without
  the paid/for-credit detail or evidence. The judge still awards a perfect score
  and falsely attributes the rule to [2], an Industry Concentration course table.
- **Exact tuition bill:** both answers improperly extend limited scholarship
  coverage. The control still acknowledges that the dollar amount is unknown;
  the candidate asserts **$0 for the cohort**. Candidate [1], financial aid
  `896:1408`, explicitly limits the scholarship to two students, while [5],
  admissions FAQ `9856:10368`, directs readers to an external fees page. This is
  an unsupported generalization despite relevant limiting context being available.
- **Off-campus conduct, online availability, admissions prerequisites:** both
  configurations still miss the annotated operative passages (0% coverage each).
  The 512 off-campus source [1] cuts the opening limiting sentence at `12608` and
  misses the operative rule at `12837:13125`; the answer draws an overbroad limit
  from that fragment. Online availability gets an evidence-bound refusal. The
  prerequisites answer again substitutes degree backgrounds for required subjects.

## Judge discrepancies that prevent a promotion decision

These are qualitative discrepancies, not replacement ratings. No corrected pass
rate is calculated from a selectively inspected subset.

1. **False candidate waiver pass:** both answers only name DS-GA 1002. Neither
   describes transcripts, highlighted courses/grades, or syllabi. The candidate
   judge gives `[2, 2]`, claiming these missing instructions are implied. Its source
   [1] contains more of the instructions than the control, but availability in the
   context is not inclusion in the answer.
2. **False control GRE pass and candidate industry/CPT credit:** the judge describes
   missing answer content as present and gold or unrelated text as retrieved.
   These discrepancies are documented above and affect both arms.
3. **False control GPA citation penalty:** its one sentence makes two claims,
   both directly supported by [3]. The rubric does not require a separate citation
   after each clause; the judge invents that restriction and converts it into a fail.
4. **Capstone refusal inconsistency:** the control says the user's project has
   not been assigned, an unsupported personal-status inference, but receives full
   grounding and abstention credit. The candidate limits itself to missing assignment
   information and receives abstention **0**, while the judge's notes praise its
   refusal and incorrectly say the score is null. The recorded pass→fail change is
   not evidence of a substantive answer regression.
5. **Tuition refusal inconsistency:** the candidate invents $0, yet receives
   abstention **2**. The judge's own explanation says the answer failed to abstain.
   It still fails overall through the other dimensions, but the refusal rating is
   unreliable.
6. **Online evidence confusion:** the candidate correctly says its sources do not
   state online availability. The judge gives groundedness 0 by treating the gold
   FAQ answer as retrieved, even though all five retrieved passages lack it.

The four recorded fail→pass cases are scope, waiver, tracks, and working enrollment;
the two pass→fail cases are career advising and personal capstone team. These
transitions mix system changes with the judgment errors above.

## Verification and next step

All 250 saved retrieved excerpts and 82 reference-span instances across both frozen
case copies were verified against their local TXT files at the recorded offsets.
Retrieval metrics were recomputed. Both runs have 25 unique successful answers and
25 valid review records, with matching answer hashes, protocol, evaluator and
generation implementation hashes, questions, corpus, and rubric. JSON artifacts,
local documentation links, and whitespace were checked. No application code changed,
so the application test suite was not rerun for this artifact-only change.

Before another parameter sweep, calibrate the evaluation on these observed
disagreements: require evidence of what the answer actually says, distinguish gold
references from retrieved sources, and review refusal scores against their notes.
Any revised judge protocol should regrade **both saved answer sets**, preserve this
round, and report a separate comparison. Human calibration should be labeled human
only when actually performed by a person. Then repeat promising configurations and
use a separately frozen unseen set before making broader quality claims.

Application defaults remain unchanged. This run tests a larger fixed-size chunk;
it does not establish that chunk-size tuning alone solves the observed retrieval
and generation problems.
